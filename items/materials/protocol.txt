Study protocol, micromobility adoption study

Design. Mixed methods, running from January to September. Two components: a
repeated cross-sectional survey of city residents, and semi-structured interviews
with a purposive subsample.

Survey. Three waves, in February, May and August, each targeting 1,200 completed
responses. Sampling is stratified by district and age band against the most recent
census distribution, with quotas set for the six districts and five age bands.
Recruitment is through an online panel provider with a supplementary intercept
sample at four transit interchanges to reduce digital-access bias. Median
completion time in wave one was eleven minutes. Wave one achieved 1,247 completed
responses with a 62 percent completion rate among those who started.

Interviews. Thirty semi-structured interviews, recruited from survey respondents
who consented to follow-up. Purposive selection across three user categories:
frequent users, occasional users, and lapsed users, with ten in each. Interviews
ran between 35 and 55 minutes, conducted remotely, recorded and transcribed with
consent. A single topic guide covered journey context, decision factors, barriers,
and comparison with alternatives, with probes used flexibly.

Analysis. Survey data analysed with descriptive statistics and logistic regression
predicting current usage from demographic and access variables, weighted to the
census distribution. Interview transcripts coded thematically, with an initial
inductive pass followed by a structured coding frame applied by two coders.
Intercoder agreement was checked on a 20 percent subsample.

Ethics. Approved by the institutional review committee. Participants consented to
recording and to anonymised quotation. No incentives beyond a prize draw entry.
