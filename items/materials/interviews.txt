PARTICIPANT 04 — daily commuter, 34, central district

I started using them because the bus was unreliable in the mornings, not because
I wanted to. The stop is a ten minute walk and then whatever comes, comes. With a
scooter I know roughly what the journey takes. It's about fifteen minutes door to
door and it varies by two or three either way.

What I didn't expect was that I stopped planning. Before, I'd check the app,
leave at a particular time. Now I just go outside and there's usually something
within a couple of streets. When there isn't, it throws the whole morning, and
that happens maybe once a fortnight. That's the thing that would push me back to
the bus if it got worse.

Cost is the part I've stopped thinking about, which is probably bad. It's about
four pounds each way and I've never sat down and worked out what that comes to.
My partner did and was not impressed.

I don't ride in the rain. If it's wet I walk to the station and take the train,
which takes longer but I'm not arriving soaked. So my usage is very seasonal,
much more than I would have guessed before I looked at my own trip history.


PARTICIPANT 11 — occasional user, 52, north district

I use them for one specific thing, which is getting to the sports centre on
Saturday mornings. It's an awkward journey otherwise. Two buses or a twenty-five
minute walk with a bag.

I wouldn't call myself a user really. Maybe forty times a year. And I only use
the bikes, not the scooters, because I don't trust myself on a scooter and I've
seen people come off them. The bikes feel like a bike. I've been riding a bike
since I was a child.

The thing I find difficult is the parking. You're meant to leave them in the
bays and the bay near the sports centre is often full, so you're circling around
looking for somewhere legitimate, which adds five minutes to a fifteen minute
journey. I've been fined once for leaving one outside a bay and I've been careful
since.

There's an age thing too. Everyone else using them looks about twenty-five. That
doesn't stop me but I notice it. If they wanted more people like me they'd need
to think about how it looks, and about the bays, and probably about a monthly
price rather than paying each time.


PARTICIPANT 19 — former user, 28, east district

I used them constantly for about eight months and then stopped almost overnight.
Two things happened. I had a near miss with a car turning across me at a junction
with no protected lane, and separately the price went up.

The near miss was the bigger one. I was fine, nothing happened, but I thought
about it for days. I'd been riding on roads I now think were not safe, and I'd
been doing it because the alternative routes took longer. After that I started
taking longer routes and the time advantage disappeared, and once the time
advantage disappeared the cost stopped making sense.

I'd go back if the lanes were there. That's the honest answer. Not cheaper
vehicles, not more vehicles. The lanes. There's a stretch of maybe eight hundred
metres near the ring road that's the problem, and everyone I know who rides has
a view about that stretch.

I do still use them on holiday, in cities where the infrastructure is better,
which tells you something. Same vehicles, same app, completely different
experience because of what's around them.
