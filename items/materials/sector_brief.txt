- Verto, Marlow and two smaller operators exited shared e-scooter markets across
  six European cities in July and August. All four cited permit and compliance
  costs, not demand; Verto's published figures showed rider numbers up 4 percent
  in its final quarter.
- Swappable battery infrastructure moved from pilot to standard in the
  Netherlands and Denmark. Operators there report servicing costs down 18 to 22
  percent, driven mainly by fewer vehicle collections.
- Insurance premiums for shared fleets rose 11 percent this quarter, the third
  consecutive rise. Insurance is now 19 percent of operating cost, second only to
  depreciation at 34 percent.
- Lyon and Gothenburg introduced mandatory parking bays with camera-based
  compliance detection in June. Pavement obstruction complaints fell 61 percent
  and 47 percent respectively in the first eight weeks.
- Cargo e-bike registrations among businesses grew 28 percent year on year,
  against 6 percent for personal micromobility. Food and pharmacy delivery
  account for most of the growth.
- Onshore folded its micromobility arm into its main ride-hailing app in
  September, keeping the fleet and retiring the standalone brand. Early figures
  show a 15 percent lift in trips.
- Regulators in France and Ontario opened consultations on minimum vehicle
  lifespans, proposing a four-year floor. Both target the replacement cycle
  rather than usage or fleet size.
