Fig 1.  Line chart. Weekly trip volume, January to December.
        Single line, low in winter, peaking in July, sharp drop from October.

Fig 2.  Stacked bar chart. Trip purpose by season, four seasons.
        Commuting stable across seasons, leisure trips triple in summer.

Fig 3.  Bar chart. Monthly active users by district, six districts.
        Central district highest throughout, north district rising from April.

Fig 4.  Scatter plot. Trip distance against trip duration, roughly 4,000 points.
        Strong positive relationship, a cluster of short slow trips near the origin.

Fig 5.  Map. Trip origin density across the city, shaded by intensity.
        Concentration around the two rail stations, sparse in outer districts.

Fig 6.  Box plot. Trip duration by vehicle type, three types.
        E-bikes longest median, e-scooters shortest, cargo bikes widest spread.

Fig 7.  Line chart. Fleet availability by hour of day, weekday against weekend.
        Weekday shows two troughs at peak times, weekend a single midday trough.

Fig 8.  Bar chart. Reported reasons for non-use, seven categories.
        Cost highest, safety concerns second, vehicle availability third.

Fig 9.  Pie chart. Operating cost breakdown, five categories.
        Depreciation largest share, insurance second, servicing third.

Fig 10. Line chart. Average revenue per vehicle per day, twelve months.
        Declining trend with a recovery in the final two months.

Fig 11. Grouped bar chart. User age bands by vehicle type, five bands.
        Under-35s dominate e-scooters, over-45s over-represented on cargo bikes.

Fig 12. Histogram. Trip distance distribution, bin width 500 metres.
        Right-skewed, mode between one and one and a half kilometres.

Fig 13. Line chart. Pavement clutter complaints before and after parking bays.
        Steady level for six months, then a marked fall after the intervention.

Fig 14. Heat map. Trip starts by hour and day of week.
        Two bright bands on weekday mornings and evenings, diffuse at weekends.

Fig 15. Bar chart. Vehicle attrition causes, four categories.
        Ordinary wear largest, vandalism second, theft third, water damage fourth.

Fig 16. Line chart. Battery swap events per vehicle per week, before and after
        infrastructure rollout. Roughly doubles after rollout, then plateaus.

Fig 17. Scatter plot. Rainfall against daily trip volume, 365 points.
        Clear negative relationship, greater spread on low-rainfall days.

Fig 18. Stacked area chart. Market share by operator, eight quarters.
        Four operators at the start, two by the end, largest share growing.

Fig 19. Bar chart. Mode substituted by micromobility trips, six modes.
        Walking highest, bus second, private car fourth.

Fig 20. Line chart. Mean vehicle age in fleet, twenty-four months.
        Rising steadily with two step drops where new vehicles were introduced.
