Working notes, micromobility adoption study

--- Session, week 3 ---

Kickoff with the transport authority team. Four people on their side: programme
lead, data analyst, two policy officers. Programme lead is the decision maker,
the analyst is the one who will actually use anything we produce.

Their stated question is whether to renew the operator permits on current terms.
Underneath that, what they actually want to know is whether micromobility is
pulling people off buses or out of cars, because the answer changes the political
case entirely. Nobody said this outright. It came out when the policy officers
started talking about the bus contract renewal in the same breath.

Decided: we ask about substituted mode explicitly in the survey rather than
inferring it from trip data. Inference from trip data was the original plan and it
would not have answered their real question.

Open question: whether we can get operator trip data at all. The analyst thinks
two of the six will share, the others will refuse on commercial grounds. We should
not design anything that depends on having it.

Action taken: drafted the substituted-mode question with a forced choice and an
other-with-text option. Tested it on five colleagues, three of them picked
"other" and wrote something that mapped to an existing category, so the categories
need better labels.

--- Session, week 5 ---

Reworked the substituted-mode question. Categories now: walked, bus, train or
tram, drove myself, got a lift, taxi or ride-hailing, would not have made the
trip. Added the last one after a colleague pointed out that induced demand is a
real category and we would otherwise be forcing people to name a mode they never
used.

Sampling discussion. Original plan was a straight online panel. Problem is that
the population most likely to be excluded from micromobility is also the
population least likely to be on an online panel, so we would systematically miss
the people the equity part of the brief cares about.

Decided: add an intercept sample at transit interchanges. Four sites, chosen to
span the district income distribution. This costs about three weeks of fieldwork
time and we have absorbed it by cutting the planned pilot from two waves to one.

Tried and abandoned: a household mail-out to boost coverage in the two outer
districts. Costed it and it came to more than the entire interview budget for a
projected 200 responses. Not proceeding.

Open question: whether the intercept sample can be weighted together with the
panel sample without producing something indefensible. The analyst on their side
has views. We should ask before wave one rather than after.

--- Session, week 6 ---

Weighting question resolved, provisionally. We treat them as one sample with a
source indicator, weight to the census distribution on district and age, and
report source as a covariate in the models. If source turns out to predict the
outcome after weighting, we report that rather than hiding it.

Ethics submission went in. Two questions came back: whether we are recording
interviews with under-18s, which we are not, and whether the prize draw
constitutes an inducement. Answered both, expecting approval next week.

Topic guide drafted for the interviews. Four sections: how they travel now, what
prompted them to try micromobility or not, what would change their behaviour, and
how it compares to what they used before. Deliberately did not include a section
on cost, because cost comes up unprompted in every pilot conversation and asking
about it directly seems to produce a rehearsed answer.

Noted for later: the pilot conversations all produced a weather answer within the
first two minutes. If that holds in the real interviews, seasonality is a bigger
part of this story than the brief assumes, and the authority's own usage figures
are annual averages that would hide it entirely.

--- Session, week 8 ---

Wave one fieldwork opened. 1,247 completed responses against a target of 1,200.
Completion rate 62 percent among starters, which is slightly better than the
panel provider's benchmark.

Problem found in the data. The district question used the authority's own
administrative district names, and roughly nine percent of respondents selected a
district that does not match their postcode. Most of the mismatches are between
the two central districts, which were merged administratively two years ago and
which residents apparently still treat as separate.

Decided: recode from postcode where available, flag the rest, report the
discrepancy. Told the analyst, who was not surprised and said the same thing
happens in their own surveys.

Open question: whether to fix the question for waves two and three, which breaks
comparability, or leave it and correct in analysis each time. Leaning toward
leaving it. Comparability across waves is worth more than a cleaner question.

Action taken: wrote the recoding script and ran it against wave one. 94 percent of
mismatches resolved from postcode. The remaining 6 percent are respondents who did
not give a postcode and those are flagged.

--- Session, week 10 ---

First look at wave one results. Headline: 31 percent of respondents have used a
shared micromobility vehicle in the past twelve months, which is higher than the
authority expected and higher than the operator-reported unique user figures would
suggest. Two possible explanations, recall error inflating self-report, or
operator figures undercounting because of shared accounts. Both are plausible and
we cannot distinguish them with what we have.

Substituted mode is the interesting one. Walking is the largest substituted mode
at 34 percent, bus second at 27 percent, private car fourth at 11 percent. This is
not what the authority wanted to hear, since the political case has been built on
car substitution.

Decided how to handle this: we present it plainly and we present the uncertainty
plainly. The walking figure in particular needs care, because substituting a walk
with a scooter ride is a health story as well as a transport story and the brief
does not ask about health at all.

Open question: whether to raise the health angle unprompted. Programme lead is
not going to like it. The analyst will see it immediately whether or not we say
it, so saying it first seems better.

--- Session, week 12 ---

Interviews started. Six done, all frequent users so far, recruitment for lapsed
users is slower because they have less reason to respond.

Early theme, consistent across all six: nobody describes cost as a decision
factor when asked directly, and everybody mentions it later without being asked.
Two participants had never calculated what they spend. One had, after their
partner did.

Second theme: infrastructure comes up as the reason for stopping or for route
choice far more than vehicle quality or availability. One participant described a
single stretch of road as the reason they stopped entirely.

Third theme, weaker: several participants describe a change in how they plan
journeys, moving from checking timetables to just going outside. This might be
the most interesting thing in the study and it is not in the brief.

Action taken: added a probe to the topic guide about journey planning behaviour.
This is a mid-study change to the instrument and it is recorded here so it appears
in the methods write-up.

Open question: whether the planning-behaviour theme is worth a section of its own
in the final report or whether it belongs in a separate output. It is not what
they are paying for.

--- Session, week 14 ---

Recruitment for lapsed users resolved by going back to the panel provider and
screening specifically. Nine of the ten now booked.

Wave two fieldwork prepared. No changes to the instrument except the addition of
one question on trip frequency in the past four weeks, which the analyst asked for
and which does not disturb anything else.

Coding frame drafted for the interviews. Inductive pass produced 40-odd codes,
collapsed to 14 in a structured frame across four groups: journey context,
decision factors, barriers, comparison to alternatives. Second coder starts on the
20 percent subsample next week.

Noted: the seasonality question from week 6 is holding up. Wave one ran in
February and wave two runs in May. If usage differs sharply between them we have
an inference problem, because the waves are supposed to measure change over time
and would instead be measuring the weather. We should have thought about this at
design stage. Options are to acknowledge it as a limitation or to add a question
about typical usage across the year and treat that as the stable measure. Leaning
toward both.

--- Session, week 16 ---

Wave two closed. 1,193 completed responses against the 1,200 target, close enough
that we are not reopening fieldwork. Completion rate 59 percent, down three points
on wave one, which the panel provider attributes to fieldwork falling across a
public holiday.

Typical-annual-usage question went in as agreed. Early read suggests it behaves
sensibly: respondents who report high current usage in May also report high
typical usage, and the correlation is strong enough that the question looks like
it is measuring what we intended.

Substituted mode figures held almost exactly between waves. Walking 34 percent in
wave one, 33 percent in wave two. Bus 27 and 27. This is reassuring for the
finding and slightly awkward for the seasonality argument, since if weather were
driving mode substitution we would expect movement between February and May.
Worth saying plainly in the report: the substitution pattern appears stable, the
volume of usage may not be.

Tried and abandoned: splitting the analysis by weather on the day of response.
The panel provider does not give us response dates at a granularity that makes
this possible, and the intercept sample dates are recorded but not comparable.
Dropped it.

Open question: whether to run any test for wave-to-wave difference formally or
just report both waves side by side. Leaning toward side by side, since the
sample is repeated cross-sectional and a difference test implies a design we do
not have.

--- Session, week 18 ---

Interview coding complete across all thirty. Second coder finished the subsample
and we resolved disagreements by discussion rather than by adjudication, which is
recorded in the methods.

The three themes from week 12 all survived the full coding. Cost-mentioned-but-
not-volunteered appears in 24 of 30 transcripts. Infrastructure-as-decisive
appears in 19, and in 8 of the 10 lapsed users, which is the strongest single
pattern in the interview data. Planning-behaviour change appears in 11, which is
fewer than the early sessions suggested but still substantial.

Decided on the planning-behaviour question from week 12. It goes in the main
report as a short section near the end, framed as an unanticipated finding rather
than as a headline. Putting it in a separate output would mean the authority never
reads it. Programme lead has been told informally and did not object.

Action taken: pulled quotes for each theme, three per theme, checked against the
consent record for quotation permission. All clear.

Open question: how much interview material goes in the main report against an
appendix. The analyst wants more, the programme lead wants less. Current plan is
three quotes per theme in the body and the full coding frame in an appendix.

--- Session, week 20 ---

Wave three fieldwork opened and closed inside the window. 1,208 completed
responses. No instrument changes from wave two.

Headline usage figure across all three waves sits between 29 and 33 percent, with
wave three at 31.4 percent. Confidence intervals overlap across all three waves,
so we report the range and note the stability rather than claiming a trend.

Ran the logistic model on the pooled data. Car substitution is not predicted well
by anything we measured, pseudo R-squared of 0.08. Spent some time deciding
whether to report a model that explains so little. Decided yes, because the null
is itself the finding: car substitution is not concentrated in an identifiable
group, which matters for how any targeting policy would be designed.

Operator data request came back. Four of six refused, as the analyst predicted in
week 3. The two who agreed offered aggregate monthly trip counts only, which does
not help with the unique-user discrepancy. Recorded as a limitation.

Decided on the health angle from week 10. It goes in, one paragraph, in the
substitution section, stated neutrally with no recommendation attached. The
analyst was told informally and was positive about it. Programme lead has not seen
it yet and this is the thing most likely to come back in review.

Action taken: report structure agreed. Findings, then limitations, then
recommendations. Drafting starts next week against a September deadline.
